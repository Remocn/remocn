_Last updated: November 18, 2026_

A screen recording looks like a screen recording because it is one: the cursor wanders, text is too
small to read on a phone, spinners and notifications sneak in, and zooms added afterwards blur the
pixels. Rounded corners and a gradient behind the window help a little. The bigger fix is to stop
recording: rebuild the screen as a scripted scene where every click is deliberate, the camera moves
to the part that matters and the text renders sharp at any zoom. Remocn ships those scenes as
components, and a coding agent such as Claude Code fills them with your UI and copy.

## The tells that give a recording away

- **The cursor hunts** for the button before clicking it.
- **The text is unreadable** on a phone, because a 1440 px window got squeezed into a feed.
- **Zooms go soft.** Scaling a recording 2× scales its pixels too.
- **Real-world noise:** a loading spinner, a half-typed search, a Slack notification, your bookmarks
  bar.
- **No hierarchy.** Everything on screen is equally loud, so the viewer doesn't know where to look.

Auto-zoom recorders (Screen Studio, Rapidemo, Camtasia and others) fix the cursor and add zooms, and
for a quick walkthrough they are the right call. They can't remove what the recording captured.

## The fix: a scripted screen, not a filmed one

<ComponentPreview name="macro-click" />

[Macro Click](/docs/product-scenes/macro-click) starts in a close-up of a site builder: a click
publishes, the status flips to Live, and the camera pulls back to show the whole window. Nothing
here was recorded. The interface is drawn by code, so the close-up is as sharp as the wide shot, the
click lands on the exact frame you choose and there is no stray notification to cut around.

If you already have a good screenshot, keep it and move the camera instead:

<ComponentPreview name="screenshot-tour" />

[Screenshot Tour](/docs/media/screenshot-tour) pushes in on regions of your screenshot one at a
time, dims the rest and labels each stop. You give it the image and the rectangles:

```tsx
<ScreenshotTour
  src="/screens/dashboard.png"
  screenshotWidth={1440}
  screenshotHeight={900}
  stops={[{ x: 40, y: 120, width: 420, height: 200, label: "Every issue, grouped by status" }]}
/>
```

Export the screenshot at 2× so close-ups stay crisp.

## Do it with an agent

Install the Remocn plugin in Claude Code (`/plugin install remocn --marketplace Remocn/remocn`) or
Codex ([Setup](/docs/guides/setup) covers both), then from your product's repo:

<GuidePrompt>

```text
I have a screen recording of <THE FLOW, e.g. "creating a project and inviting a teammate">.
Instead of using the recording, rebuild that flow as a scripted scene with remocn
components (remocn.dev). Read the source of these screens so labels and layout match:
<FILES OR SCREENSHOTS>. Every click deliberate, typing at a human pace, the camera
pushing in on the one element each step is about. No loading states, no notifications.
1920×1080, 60fps, about 20 seconds.
```

</GuidePrompt>

The [Product demo](/docs/guides/product-demo) guide turns this into a full ~50-second demo with a
setup line, payoff and close.

## When to keep the recording

- **You need to prove it's real,** with live data, real latency or a bug report. A rebuild is a
  depiction, not evidence.
- **The video is internal** (a Loom for a teammate). Polish isn't worth it.
- **Your app changes daily and the walkthrough has 40 steps.** An auto-zoom recorder is faster for
  long tutorials.

<Faq>

**Is a rebuilt screen dishonest?**
It shows your real interface with the noise removed, the same way a marketing screenshot is cleaned
up. Keep labels and flows true to the product. If the video claims performance, show the real thing.

**Can I mix both?**
Yes. A Remotion video can play a recorded clip with `OffthreadVideo` next to scripted scenes, so you
can open on a real recording and cut to a scripted close-up.

</Faq>
