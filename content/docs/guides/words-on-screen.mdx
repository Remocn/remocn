On a landing page the reader controls the clock — they can reread. In a video the line is gone in two seconds. Writing for the screen is its own small craft, and this page is all of it.

## The rules<GuideCount n={6} />

<WordsRules
  pairs={[
    { dont: "Every launch needs a video, yours shouldn't take a week", do: "Yours shouldn't take a week" },
    { dont: "Incredibly simple", do: "Ships in one command" },
    { dont: "A huge library", do: "110+ components" },
  ]}
  icons={["lines", "shield", "mic"]}
>

- **Six words or fewer** — If a line doesn't fit, it's two beats — or it's too clever.
- **Verbs over adjectives** — "Ships in one command" beats "incredibly simple".
- **Concrete over grand** — Numbers, commands, names: "110+ components", not "a huge library".
- **One idea per line** — Split sentences; don't compress them.
- **No adjectives you can't prove**
- **Read it aloud once** — If you stumble, the viewer will too.

</WordsRules>

## Formulas for common beats<GuideCount n={5} />

<WordsFormulas>

**Pain lines** — Name the problem in the viewer's words, not yours; the second line twists or promises a way out.

> "Every launch needs a video" / "Yours shouldn't take a week"

**Taglines** — What it is plus who it's for, zero filler.

> "Cinematic video components for React"

**Positioning** — Borrow a shape people already know; nothing transfers a mental model faster.

> "Like shadcn/ui, for video"

**Value claims** — Three, each concrete, each provable.

> "110+ components" / "One command to install" / "The code is yours"

**The close** — The single shortest next action. A command *or* a URL — one, not both.

> &lt;YOUR CTA&gt;

</WordsFormulas>

## The two-second test

Whatever you're filling in — a worksheet, a prompt, a rewrite — say each line out loud at speaking pace. Longer than two seconds to say? Tighten it.
