## Usage

`TinyPlanet` draws a seeded two-row skyline with lit windows and spires on a
flat horizon, then bends it into a circle like the tiny planet effect in
360° photography. The strip is exactly as long as the planet's circumference,
so it closes without a seam. While it bends the planet shrinks to fit the
frame and the sun drifts up and to the right. Once closed, the planet turns at
a steady rate for as long as the sequence lasts.

```tsx
import { TinyPlanet } from "@/components/remocn/tiny-planet";

export const Hometown = () => (
  <TinyPlanet buildings={32} seed={9} sunColor="#F5B83D" />
);
```

`buildings` is the front row count (minimum 4); the back row gets 80% of
that. `seed` changes widths, heights, spires and windows. `spin` is the final
turning rate in degrees per frame at 30 fps, and `spin={0}` leaves the planet
still once it closes.

## Timing

At 30 fps and `speed={1}`:

| Frames | Motion |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 | The flat skyline holds |
| 8–40 | The skyline bends into a planet on an ease-in-out, fastest around frame 24 |
| 26–40 | The spin eases in while the strip closes |
| 40+ | The planet turns at `spin` degrees per frame for as long as the sequence lasts |

`length` covers the bend. Give the sequence a few extra seconds if you want
the turning planet to hold behind a title. `speed` scales the whole timeline,
including the spin rate.
