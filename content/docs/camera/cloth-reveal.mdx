## Usage

`ClothReveal` covers its children with a sheet of cloth. The sheet rests on a
soft bump in the middle, as if draped over the hidden object, with seeded
folds and waves so it never looks ironed. On the tug frame a hand pinches one
corner, lifts it, and yanks it off-frame with growing speed. The rest of the
sheet follows as a mass-spring cloth: it peels up, folds over to show its
darker underside, flutters, and casts a soft shadow on the content below.

```tsx
import { ClothReveal } from "@/components/remocn/cloth-reveal";

export const Unveil = () => (
  <ClothReveal direction="right" clothColor="#1F3A93" undersideColor="#0E1C4A">
    <YourLogo />
  </ClothReveal>
);
```

Children are centered in the frame on top of `background`. `direction` picks
the corner and the pull:

| `direction` | Corner | Pull |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `right` | Top right | Right and slightly up |
| `left` | Top left | Left and slightly up |
| `up` | Top right | Up and to the right |
| `down` | Bottom left | Down and to the left |

`delay` is the tug frame at 30 fps. `seed` changes the fold pattern and
waves of the resting sheet.

`clothColor` and `undersideColor` are parsed as hex (`#RGB` or `#RRGGBB`)
to light the cloth per vertex, so CSS names, `rgb()` and gradients are not
supported.

## Timing

At 30 fps, `speed={1}` and the default `delay` of 6:

| Frames | Motion |
| --- | --- |
| 0–6 | The sheet rests over the content |
| 6–15 | The corner lifts as the pull starts |
| 15–28 | The yank speeds up; the sheet peels back, folds and flutters |
| 28–34 | The trailing edge clears the frame |
| 34+ | The content holds, uncovered |

The cloth is simulated for 72 frames at 30 fps, which covers any `delay` up to
about 38. `speed` scales the timeline after the simulation, so it does not
change how the cloth moves.

This is one of the heavier components in the registry. The simulation, a
40×24 grid with 12 constraint passes per step, runs once per composition size,
`direction`, `delay` and `seed`. Each frame then draws about 900 shaded SVG
quads through two blur filters. Expect slower renders than a CSS-only reveal,
and use it once as the payoff rather than on every scene.
