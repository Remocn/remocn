## Usage

`DollyZoom` renders a perspective corridor with a red door at the far end and
a standing figure in the middle. Over the move the camera dollies toward the
figure while the focal length shortens at exactly the same rate, so the figure
never changes size while the corridor stretches away behind it, the way the
shot works in film. Floor tiles, ceiling lights and frame lines fade with
distance, and a vignette darkens the corners.

```tsx
import { DollyZoom } from "@/components/remocn/dolly-zoom";

export const Vertigo = () => <DollyZoom />;
```

`intensity` sets how far the camera travels, from 0 (no move) to 2. At 1 the
camera ends at about 37% of its starting distance. `reverse` plays the same
move backward: the corridor starts stretched and compresses toward the
subject.

### Your own subject

Children replace the figure. They are centered in the frame and are not
scaled, which is what keeps them steady.

```tsx
import { Img, staticFile } from "remotion";
import { DollyZoom } from "@/components/remocn/dolly-zoom";

export const LogoVertigo = () => (
  <DollyZoom intensity={1.4} doorColor="#3D7BFF">
    <Img src={staticFile("logo.svg")} style={{ height: 160 }} />
  </DollyZoom>
);
```

The built-in figure stands on the floor. Children sit at the frame center
instead, so a tall subject may overlap the floor line; that is usually fine
for a logo or a word.

## Timing

At 30 fps and `speed={1}`:

| Frames | Motion |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4 | The shot holds on the starting framing |
| 4–36 | The camera dollies in while zooming out on one eased curve, fastest around frame 16 |
| 36+ | The shot holds on the stretched corridor |

`speed` scales the move, so `speed={2}` ends at frame 18.
