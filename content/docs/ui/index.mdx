## What makes this different from regular shadcn

In a browser, a button enters its loading state because the user clicked. In a video, it enters that state because you authored `{ at: 35, state: "loading" }`. The component looks identical; the wiring is completely different.

This also means you get things that are impossible in a real UI: scrub backward through a form fill, play a button click at half speed, or loop a checkbox toggle indefinitely, all without any special setup.

## Two kinds of atom

Every primitive is one of two shapes. Knowing which tells you where the frame is read and how timing reaches the component.

</AtomsSection>

<AtomCards>

<AtomCard
  title="State atoms"
  meta="Snap or smooth"
  code={`{ at: 12, state: "hover" }`}
  items={["button", "input", "checkbox", "switch"]}
>
  Pure visual functions of state and theme. They never read the frame; the state comes from a prop, `useCurrentState`, or a transition hook like `useButtonTransition`.
</AtomCard>

<AtomCard
  title="Motion atoms"
  meta="Loop on the frame"
  code="<Caret blink={!tw.typing} />"
  items={["spinner", "caret", "typing-indicator"]}
>
  Loop on `useCurrentFrame()` with no discrete states. No `Date`, no `Math.random`, no `requestAnimationFrame`, so they stay safe in the headless renderer.
</AtomCard>

</AtomCards>
